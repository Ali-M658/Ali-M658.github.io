How it works

AI Relay lives in Chrome's side panel, next to the chats you already have open. It does nothing until you ask it to.

  1. Pick your chats

    Mark each open AI tab as a source to summarize, a destination to receive the result, or both. Choose a summary style, from a three-line gist to a full handoff, or write your own prompt.

  2. Collect the summaries

    AI Relay types your summary request into each source chat and reads back the reply. You can watch each chat's progress, and retry or skip one that stalls.

  3. Review, then insert

    Edit, reorder or drop each summary. Insert fills the other chat's message box and switches you to it, so you read it and press Enter yourself. It never sends on its own.

Or read the answer right here

If you'd rather not switch tabs, choose what the other chat should do with the context, such as suggest next steps or check for mistakes, and confirm. AI Relay sends it and shows the reply in the panel. This is the only action that sends a message for you, and it always asks first.

Built to stay out of your way

There's no account, no server and no tracking. AI Relay is a tool you steer, not an agent that acts on its own.

Nothing leaves your browser

Summaries are kept in the panel while you work. They're saved only if you turn that on, and you can clear them any time.

Access you can revoke

One permission prompt covers the supported AI sites, and nothing else. Revoke any site, or pause the extension, from Settings.

Your drafts are safe

If a chat already has an unsent message in its box, AI Relay skips that chat instead of overwriting it.

One thing to know: collecting adds a turn to each source chat, because the summary request and its reply are ordinary messages. If you use a chatbot with limited credits or message caps, this counts toward them. It shouldn't be much.
